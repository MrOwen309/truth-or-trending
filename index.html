You'll see posts drawn at random from a bank of . They come from news outlets, politicians, bloggers, government agencies, family, friends, and strangers. Each time you play, you'll get a different mix.

What the verdicts mean

The fact-checker's toolkit: SIFT

S — StopNotice your emotions. Posts that make you angry, scared, or excited are designed to be shared fast.
I — Investigate the sourceWho is behind this? What's their expertise, track record, or agenda?
F — Find better coverageRead "laterally." What do other sources say?
T — Trace claimsFollow quotes, images, and numbers back to the original.

How scoring works

  • Use the three investigation tools to gather evidence. Not every clue matters, and some are distractions.
  • Choose your verdict: 10 points if it's right. (Advanced mode: mixing up False and Misleading earns half credit, 5 points, because you still spotted that the post can't be trusted.)
  • Star the one piece of evidence that best proves your verdict:
    🎯 Smoking gun (the single most decisive clue) = +5 · Helpful but not the best = +2 · Not decisive = +0
  • Evidence points count only if your verdict earns full or half credit.

⚠️ Every person, organization, website, and event in this game is fictional, even though the situations are realistic. Don't Google them. Use only the evidence in the game.
